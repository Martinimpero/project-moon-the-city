# PROJECT MOON: THE CITY
### A Roleplaying Game in the City
#### GM & Player Core Rulebook

---

## Welcome to the City

The City runs on paperwork, debt, and things it doesn't explain.

Somewhere above everyone's heads, the Head sets the rules and steps in only when one gets broken. Wings — corporations built around a Singularity, a piece of something not quite of this world — offer salaries nobody can walk away from and secrets nobody asked to keep. Syndicates run the Backstreets on favors and fear. Offices keep Fixers working, one quota and one cut at a time. And somewhere between all of it, Fixers get hired to handle the things too small for the Head to notice and too dangerous for anyone else to touch.

You are not soldiers, and this is not a war story. You are people with rent to pay, people to protect, and a past that isn't done with you yet. Project Moon: The City is a game about ordinary people doing extraordinary, dangerous, morally compromised work inside a City that was never built to be lived in comfortably — and about what that work does to who they are.

Combat happens. It is rare, costly, and never the whole answer. More often, the game is a negotiation, an investigation, a favor called in at the worst possible time, a conversation you can't afford to lose. When violence does come, it means something, because everything in this game is built to make sure it does.

## What You Need to Play

- Three to five players and one Game Master (GM).
- A pool of ten-sided dice (d10) — six to ten per player is comfortable.
- A character sheet for each player (a blank one is in the Appendix).
- Paper or index cards for tracking Clocks, Bonds, and Contracts.
- Two to four hours for a session, though scenes are built to compress or stretch as the table needs.

No miniatures, no battle grid, and no combat-turn tracker beyond what's described in Part VI. Everything here is designed to run at a table with dice, character sheets, and conversation.

## The Tone of the Game

This is a game about competent people in over their heads, not superheroes. A character who is very good at their job can still lose a fight, misjudge a client, or watch a Bond break because they chose the mission over the person. The City doesn't reward heroics with safety. It notices you, and it remembers.

Expect dark humor sitting right next to real horror. Expect corporate absurdity — a form in triplicate standing between a character and something that matters, a Wing's HR department handling a supernatural incident with the same tone as a workplace injury. Expect Abnormalities that are rarely monsters to be killed and much more often mysteries to be understood, contained, or survived. And expect the psychological cost of this life to be as real and as trackable as a physical wound.

The rules that follow exist to make all of that playable, not just describable.

Before you play, read Part VIII §11 (Session Zero and Safety). This game is built to hurt a little, on purpose, and everyone at the table should know how to stop it.

## Quick Start

**You need:** a GM, three to five players, a handful of d10s, one pregenerated character each (Appendix L), and half an hour of reading. GMs: read Part VIII §11 (Session Zero and Safety) first.

**1. The roll.** Add an Attribute and a Skill; roll that many d10s. Each 7, 8, 9 or 10 is a **Success**. The GM names a **Difficulty** aloud first (1 routine, 2 hard, 3 the City pushing back). Successes at Difficulty + 2: **Critical**. At Difficulty: **Success**. One short: **Partial** (yes, but... or no, and...). Less: **Failure**. No Successes and a 1 on any die: **Critical Failure**. Every roll changes something.

**2. E.G.O.** Your E.G.O. equals your Resolve. Before rolling, spend points one-for-one as extra dice; a spent die showing a 1 or 2 brings a **Complication**. Below half, the first 1 you roll each scene is a **Flashpoint**: your Vice takes the wheel for a beat. Rest returns a little E.G.O.; when the GM invokes your Vice, you get 1 back.

**3. Stress and the Wound.** **Stress** (0 to 5) rises when your Wound is hit, and shrinks your E.G.O. When E.G.O. would hit 0 at the moment your **Boundary** is on the line, it's a **Hail Mary**: a voice says what your Wound has always said about you, and you answer with who you are. Roll Resolve + a Skill. You Manifest your E.G.O., or you Distort. The table plays it out.

**4. Sins.** Your **Resonance** says what your Wound feels like. **Tag** a roll with a Sin you are 2+ in and say why. Against another Sin, strong is +1 die and weak is −1. The wheel: Sloth, Wrath, Pride, Envy, Gluttony, Lust, Gloom, and back; each beats the next two. **Ride** a Sin for a scene: its Favored Skills get +1 die, its Hindered Skills −2. Your starting gear is attuned to a Sin.

**5. Bonds and Clocks.** A **Bond** ties you to a person: spend a point for a die, to upgrade a Partial, or to ease Stress. A **Clock** (4, 6 or 8 segments) tracks pressure: one segment per setback, never more than two.

**6. Fights.** They are rare: say it aloud ("this is a fight now"). Everyone acts once per **Exchange**. In an opposed roll the one trying to change things needs more Successes. **Harm** has four tiers: Hurt, Injured (−1 die), Wounded (−2), Maimed. You can **concede** before it's too late.

**7. Your first session.** The GM prepares one Contract (Part VIII §3) or runs the Session 01 kit. Open by asking what each character is carrying; close by naming what ticked. Then read Part II (the roll), Part IV (the Self) and Part X (the Sins).
