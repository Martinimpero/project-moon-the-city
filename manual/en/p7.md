# PART VII — THE CITY TOOLKIT

This Part is a kit of parts, not a gazetteer. Nothing here needs to exist before a campaign starts — build each piece during prep, or invent it on the spot when the fiction asks for a name. Keep a running list of what's been established rather than pre-building the whole City; your table's City should look different from every other table's by session three.

## 1. Districts

**Template**

- **Name:**
- **Dominant Institution:** (Head, Syndicate, Office, or Wing — whichever shapes daily life here most visibly)
- **Texture:** (two or three sensory details)
- **Tension:** (an unresolved conflict already in motion before the PCs arrive)
- **Clock:** (already ticking, tied to the Tension — see Part II §4)

A District doesn't need borders or a map position until the fiction actually requires one.

> **EXAMPLE**
> *The Backstreets — Threadmill Row.* Dominant Institution: Syndicate. Texture: stacked market stalls under permanently damp scaffolding; the smell of frying oil and machine coolant. Tension: two Syndicate crews contesting the same stretch of black-market trade. Clock: a 6-segment "Open War" Clock, at 2 segments from a recent knifing.

## 2. Wings

A Wing's District has two faces: the **Nest**, its corporate core, and its share of **Backstreets** around it — both bound by the same house Taboos the Wing sets for its own territory.

**Template**

- **Industry:** (the public-facing business)
- **Singularity Connection:** (what unstable or extraordinary force actually powers the Wing's work — usually classified even from most of its own employees)
- **Employment Flavor:** (what an Employment Bond with this Wing feels like — protective, controlling, seductive, or some mix)
- **Secret:** (something hidden even from compliant employees — this is where most campaign hooks live)
- **Whose suffering?:** every Singularity runs on human suffering. Who pays for this Wing's, and does the Wing's staff know?

**GM Advice.** Keep the Singularity Connection vague until the fiction demands specifics — over-explaining an Abnormality's nature too early costs you mystery you can't get back.

> **EXAMPLE**
> *Halcyon Wing.* Industry: pharmaceuticals and grief counseling. Singularity Connection: a proprietary treatment derived from something recovered, not synthesized. Employment Flavor: genuinely generous — Wing Support here means real trauma counseling, which makes the Bond harder to want to spend against them. Secret: the treatment's source material is a contained Abnormality nobody current on staff has been told about.

## 3. Fixers and Associations

A Fixer is licensed and graded by an **Association**. There are twelve formal Associations (the Hana Association is the largest and most trusted; invent others as the table needs, such as the **Rill Exchange** below). Each has a specialty, four regional Branches, and six Sections that sort work and enforce contracts. A Fixer takes Contracts through an **Office**, or, rarely and riskily, alone. An **Associate Office** gets steady work and a salary but can't refuse an assignment; an independent chooses freely, and nobody much trusts one.

**The Report.** Every Contract ends with a report to the Association: the crew decides what to tell it. An honest report banks progress toward the next Grade (Part IX). A falsified one is faster and pays better, but opens a Heat Clock (4) on the Association; when it fills, the Association sends someone to ask questions. Breaking a brokered contract costs standing outright, and the Association may send a Taboo Hunter or an enforcer.

**Template**

- **Reputation:** (how the other three institutions regard it)
- **Job Flow:** (what kind of Contracts it typically routes — recovery, courier, "problem-solving")
- **House Rules:** (its own code — violating it costs standing, Part V §2)

> **EXAMPLE**
> *The Rill Exchange.* Reputation: quietly trusted by Offices, avoided by Syndicates who see it as competition. Job Flow: mostly recovery and negotiation work, rarely anything openly violent. House Rule: never take a job against another registered Fixer — breaking this tanks standing City-wide, not just with the Exchange.

## 4. Syndicates

Most Syndicates hold a few streets. The five that matter most — the **Five Fingers** — carry enough weight to rank as Stars of the City, on par with a Wing.

**Template**

- **Territory:** (which District, or part of one, it controls)
- **Racket:** (its primary illicit trade)
- **Recruitment Style:** (usually Debt or Obligation Bonds)
- **Escalation Ladder:** three concrete steps of what happens as a Heat Clock against this Syndicate fills — a warning, a response, a reckoning — so the GM always has the next beat ready.
- **Terms:** how it describes itself (a Family, an Association, a Fist), its protection money, and its Rats (the unrated foot soldiers a Grade 9 might clash with).
- **Flavor:** each Finger has a style: the **Thumb** is hierarchy, the **Index** writes Prescripts that bind members, the **Middle** nurses vengeance, the **Ring** is art and show, the **Pinky** infiltrates. Raids on rival Offices generate Fixer work.

> **EXAMPLE**
> *The Coldwater Concern.* Territory: Threadmill Row. Racket: black-market Abnormality-adjacent salvage. Recruitment: Debt, aggressively offered to anyone visibly struggling. Escalation Ladder: (1) a visible warning — vandalism, a follow; (2) a direct confrontation, Pressure-based, not yet lethal; (3) the fight the table has been building toward, using the group-combat rules (Part VI §6).

**GM Advice.** Write the three-step ladder before you need it, for any Syndicate the campaign is likely to return to.

## 5. Offices

An Office is a small Fixer-employing business — most working Fixers belong to one, and an Office in turn answers to a larger Association above it. This is where the daily grind of the work actually lives: who gets which job, what cut comes off the top, whether the person doing the assigning still trusts you.

**Template**

- **Function:** (the kind of Fixer work it specializes in routing — recovery, courier, background checks on nervous clients)
- **The Grind:** (one specific, recurring professional pressure that comes with working under this Office — a cut, a quota, a house rule enforced pettily)
- **The Person Behind the Desk:** (a single named NPC who embodies the Office — sympathetic, hostile, or simply exhausted)

Offices rarely generate Combat — they're the natural home for Negotiation-flavored Social Conflict scenes (Part V §6).

> **EXAMPLE**
> *The Ninth Registry.* Function: intake and dispatch for freelance Fixers not yet attached to a full Association. The Grind: a 30% cut off every payment before it reaches the Fixer, calculated by a formula nobody outside the Registry has ever seen written down. The Person Behind the Desk: Clerk Anwen Sorel, who hates the cut as much as anyone and will quietly round a job up to a better Risk tier for anyone who treats her like a person instead of an obstacle.

**GM Advice.** An Office scene works best as connective tissue between higher-stakes beats, not as the focus of a whole session.

## 6. Abnormalities

An Abnormality encounter is built from behavior and consequence, not a stat block. A well-designed Abnormality should rarely, if ever, reach Part VI's Combat rules — Combat is what happens when every safer Approach has failed, not the default plan.

**Template**

- **Origin:** whose will, desire, or fear it carries. An Abnormality is a fragment of someone's subconscious, so it wants what they wanted and fears what they feared. Every Approach should work, or fail, because of its Origin.
- **Alignment:** the Sin it embodies, with a rating from its Class (Part X §3 and §8).
- **Containment State:** Contained, Breaching, or Loose.
- **Behavior:** what it does under normal, unprovoked conditions — often unsettling rather than violent.
- **Trigger:** the specific condition that changes its behavior — a sound, a lie told near it, being looked at directly. Never simply "when attacked."
- **Breach Effect:** what happens if the Trigger is set off. This is where Harm, Trauma, or a Clock enters play — not necessarily a fight.
- **Breach Clock:** a Clock (usually 3 or 4 segments) for how close it is to breaking containment. A bad Approach result, a Complication near it, or a Trigger fills a segment. When it fills, it Breaches and the Breach Effect happens.
- **Approach:** four ways a character might interact with it, each favoring a different pairing: **Instinct** (Body or Resolve), **Insight** (Mind), **Attachment** (Presence), and **Repression** (Body or Resolve, with a Combat skill). Instinct, Insight and Attachment are the safe Approaches. **Repression** is suppressing it by force: it holds it still, takes a Harm-equivalent wound or Stress each Exchange it's held, and removes a segment from the Breach Clock on a Success. It is the last resort before the fight.

**When everything fails.** If Repression fails and the Breach Clock fills, the Abnormality Breaches and the table is left with two options: **fight** (Part VI), which an Abnormality of high Class is rarely worth, or **flee**, run as a chase (Part VI §7) against the Abnormality's reach. Neither ends the Abnormality; it can't be killed, only driven back to containment. Giving up and leaving is a valid outcome.

**Observation.** Learning an Abnormality takes four layers, each earned by one Insight success: **I** Basic Info (what it is), **II** Managerial Guidelines (what upsets it), **III** Work Preferences (which Approach it likes), **IV** Escape Info (how it breaks out). Each layer gives a die or removes a Complication on the next Approach.

**Dread.** Abnormalities are rated by **Class**, from ZAYIN (a nuisance) up to ALEPH (a threat to the City): ZAYIN is about Grade 9–8, TETH 7–6, HE 5–4, WAW 3–2, ALEPH 1. On first sight of an Abnormality whose Class outranks your Grade, you lose 1 to 2 E.G.O. (the same taken E.G.O. as Part IV). Damage from a Breach can be physical (Harm), mental (Stress or E.G.O.), or both at once (call it *Black*). A *Pale* effect ignores armor.

> **EXAMPLE**
> *The Waiting Room.* Containment: Breaching. Behavior: an empty room that always has exactly one more chair than the number of people who entered it. Trigger: sitting in the extra chair. Breach Effect: whoever sits vanishes from the fiction until a Reckoning-adjacent scene (Part IV §7) is played out to retrieve them — no Harm roll, no Combat, just a Trauma trigger for anyone who watched it happen. Approach: Insight (Mind+Investigation) can work out the counting pattern before anyone sits; Attachment (Presence+Empathy) can convince the room, if it's aware at all, that everyone present already belongs.

## 7. The Head, the Eye, and the Claw

Three entities sit at the true top of the City, each ruling one of the three central Districts directly: **the Head**, **the Eye**, and **the Claw**. None of them are run as an NPC, none of them roll dice, and none of them are ever fought.

**The Head** sets the City's Taboos — the handful of absolute laws every District has to answer to, distinct from the smaller house Taboos a Wing sets for its own Nest. Breaking a Wing's own Taboo brings that Wing's own enforcers down on you. Breaking one of the Head's is close to a death sentence. The Head only steps in for the second kind, or for a threat serious enough to be called an Impurity of the City — everything smaller, it lets the City handle on its own. A dangerous Abnormality sitting quietly in a Backstreet breaks nobody's Taboo, and draws nothing.

**The Eye** watches. Its agents, **Beholders**, are the City's surveillance made concrete — they don't punish, they notice, and being noticed is the warning.

**The Claw** punishes. Its agents, **Claws**, carry out sentences on anyone who breaks a Taboo, with no appeal.

**Arbiters** answer to the Head alone, called in only for the worst-case Impurity of the City — a threat that outstrips anything a single Wing, Syndicate, or Color Fixer could handle.

At the table, all three are represented through the same tools:

- **Directives** — short, absolute instructions that arrive through institutional channels: a reassignment, a mandatory procedure, an unexplained restriction. PCs can resist only indirectly, through the institutions that carry them out.
- **Redactions** — information that was available and now isn't, signaling that someone above has taken an interest without saying so.
- **The City-wide Notice Clock** — a slow, campaign-length Clock the GM fills privately from major PC actions that draw real attention. Its completion reshapes the campaign's status quo; it doesn't end the campaign in a single climactic fight.

> **EXAMPLE**
> After a campaign-long pattern of PCs interfering with Wing operations, the GM privately fills the last segment of the Notice Clock. The response isn't a boss fight — it's a Directive: every Wing in the City receives a simultaneous, unexplained personnel audit, and three of the PCs' Bonds inside those Wings quietly stop returning calls. No Taboo was actually broken, so it stops there — a warning, not an Arbiter.

**GM Advice.** The temptation to give the Head, the Eye, or the Claw a voice or a face at some climactic moment is exactly the move to resist. Their power comes from staying unseen — and from the fact that they mostly don't bother.

## 8. Worked Example

The GM preps a new District — *Fenwick Undercroft* — using the template in §1: Dominant Institution, Office; Texture, a converted rail tunnel repurposed as cramped dispatch offices, fluorescent light over damp brick; Tension, the Ninth Registry has quietly been rerouting a slice of Fixer payouts to an account that doesn't exist; Clock, a 4-segment "Discrepancy Noticed" Clock, already at 1 segment.

A Fixer crew, sent by the Rill Exchange to investigate the missing payouts, discovers the Undercroft's basement holds an Abnormality — *The Filing*: Containment, Loose; Behavior, sorts anything paper-based left near it into an order only it understands; Trigger, removing something from its sorted order; Breach Effect, the remover's own paperwork — permits, contracts, identity records — begins quietly rewriting itself elsewhere in the City.

One Fixer tries Insight (Mind+Investigation) to understand its sorting logic before touching anything, and succeeds — avoiding the Breach entirely. The session ends with a solved mystery, a new Bond with Clerk Sorel, and the Discrepancy Clock defused rather than triggered.

## 9. The Night and the Taboos

**The Night.** Every night for about eighty minutes (roughly 3:13 to 4:34), the Backstreets lose almost all law. Two rules hold: **residential areas are not touched**, and **nothing may be recorded** (only eyewitness testimony counts, and reporting what happened draws the Claw). **Sweepers**, sanctioned by the Head, come in three waves and consume everything outdoors. Criminals use the Night to dispose of people.

Run it as a **Clock of three segments, one per wave**. Wave 1 thins the streets. Wave 2 consumes whatever is outdoors. Wave 3 clears what's left. A safe room is a residential building; a crew caught outside when Wave 2 hits is in danger. Contract hooks: retrieve something before the last wave; a witness who can't testify; someone taken out into the Night to be lost.

**Taboos.** The Head's Taboos are specific, and the examples are what a table remembers: human-like machines and AI, resurrection, cloning beyond seven days, limits on firearms, tax evasion, violating the Night's rules. A Wing may add its own **house Taboos** for its District. **Taboo Hunters** are Fixers hired by Wings: their Grade matches the severity of the violation, their priority is to *stop the infraction and recover the evidence*, lethal force is allowed, and in another Wing's District they need local authorization. A Taboo hunt is a Contract archetype.

## 10. Living in the City

- **Feathers and permits.** Nests are made of **feathers**, the people the Wing protects, taxes and may evict. A **migration permit** from the Backstreets is a dream prize, and a leash: a Wing revokes it. Pay for a job with one and you buy the Fixer's loyalty with a Debt.
- **Augmentation.** Nearly every Fixer has some. Nest augments are good and expensive; Backstreets augments are cheap and have side effects. Only the brain stays organic. An augment is a Debt (a Bond to its maker) and may add a Complication when its quality fails. The rules are in Part VI §5.
- **Workshops.** Workshops make gear, are approved by the Tres Association, and run on patents. Top shops choose their clients, and a no-name Grade 9 may be refused.
- **Money.** Ahn is the currency. Most people spend nearly all of it on living costs and taxes. For play, treat upkeep as a standing drain: Office cut, taxes, augment maintenance. In play this is Resources (Part VI §5).
- **The Outskirts.** Beyond the City's walls, the Head's dumping ground for Impurities and what it won't have inside. Fallen NPCs and unwanted people end up there.
Part VIII covers how the GM runs all of this at the table, session to session.
