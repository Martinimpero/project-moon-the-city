# PART V — SOCIAL PLAY

## 1. Bonds

A **Bond** ties a character to another person. Each has a Type — Trust, Fear, Debt, Affection, Obligation, or Hatred — and a Strength from 1 to 3. A character can hold more than one Bond, of different flavors, with the same person at once: a boss can be both an Obligation 2 and, quietly, an Affection 1.

**Spending a Bond.** Spend a point of Bond Strength to do one of the following:

- Add one bonus die to a roll where that relationship plausibly helps.
- Upgrade a Partial result to a full Success, once per scene.
- Resist a point of Stress that the relationship would reasonably buffer against.

Spending a Bond doesn't destroy it, but it doesn't regenerate on its own — rebuilding it takes an actual scene where the relationship is tended to, not a downtime checkbox.

**Bonds run both ways.** Every Bond a player character holds on an NPC, the GM may also track in reverse — what that NPC holds on the PC. The GM spends an NPC-side Bond exactly like a player spends one: to call in a favor, apply leverage, or justify a betrayal that isn't arbitrary.

**Growing and decaying Bonds.** A Bond grows by +1 Strength (maximum 3) through a dedicated relationship scene — table time actually spent on the relationship. A Bond decays when spent hard or repeatedly without tending, at the GM's call, and optionally from long stretches of neglect.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A Fixer has Debt 2 on a corporate lawyer she once helped. The GM privately notes the lawyer also quietly fears what she knows about him — a Fear 1 Bond running the other direction. Months later, when he needs her to disappear for a while, the GM spends it: a favor she never agreed to, but can't easily refuse.

## 2. Faction Standing

A character's standing with any Association, Wing, Syndicate, or Office is the sum of their Bond Strengths with named individuals inside it — there's nothing new to track. Standing falls into rough tiers:

| Standing | Tier |
|---|---|
| 0 | Unknown |
| 1–2 | Known |
| 3–5 | Trusted |
| 6+ | Made (rare, usually campaign-defining) |

Standing doesn't grant dice bonuses on its own — it's a narrative-access gate: what doors open, what contracts get offered, who returns a call. The real mechanical leverage in a crisis still comes from spending the specific Bond underneath it.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A character with Trust 2 on a Fixer dispatcher and Obligation 1 on a senior Fixer has standing 3 with that Association — Trusted. She gets offered better contracts unprompted, but if she needs a favor right now, she still has to spend one of the actual Bonds.

## 3. The Four Institutions and How They Press

Part I §3 covers the Head, Syndicates, Offices, and Wings at a glance. Mechanically, each presses through the tools above: the Head through Clocks and consequence, never a roll against a PC directly; Syndicates through Debt and Obligation Bonds; Offices through quotas, cuts, and standing with whoever assigns the work; and Wings through Employment Bonds that grant Wing Support — reduced Harm severity, a small recovery bonus during sanctioned downtime, and stronger starting Bonds with coworkers — in exchange for costing more than usual to spend against the Wing itself. Associations, which sit above Offices, press through the license and the Report (Part VII §3).

## 4. Contracts

Most jobs — Fixer work or otherwise — take the shape of a **Contract**, with five parts:

- **Client** — who, and what they actually want, which may not be what they say.
- **Objective** — the concrete goal.
- **Payment** — what's offered. Payment is paid in **Resources** (Part VI §5): each PC earns Resources equal to the Risk rating, minus the Office's cut at Risk 3 or 4. What the client offers beyond that (a favor, a permit, a door opened) is told in the fiction.
- **Complication Clause** — an agreed "if this goes wrong" consequence, usually tied to starting a Heat Clock.
- **Risk rating** — 1 to 4, mapping directly to Difficulty (Part II §1).

Accepting a Contract as offered is always an option. Negotiating it — pushing payment up, scope down, or the Complication Clause softer — is a Social Conflict scene in its own right, and pushing too hard costs standing with the client even on a success.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A Fixer Office is offered a Risk 2 Contract: recover something from a Backstreets address, no questions. As offered, the Complication Clause puts all the Heat on the Fixers if it goes loud. Negotiating that down to shared Heat is a Presence+Persuasion roll against the client's Presence+Deception — the client is clearly hiding how much this actually matters to them.

## 5. Heat and Institutional Attention

When a faction's suspicion or attention builds over multiple scenes, track it on a **Clock** (Part II §4), typically 4 to 8 segments depending on how fast it should build. Fill a segment for a botched job, a triggered Complication Clause, or a pattern of behavior the fiction makes notice-worthy. A full Heat Clock is a concrete institutional response — an audit, a visit, a contract quietly pulled, a Syndicate enforcer at the door — never vague, and never automatically fatal.

A character or crew can run more than one Heat Clock at once — separate Heat with a Wing and a Syndicate, for instance.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A Fixer crew working against a Wing's interests accumulates Heat on a 6-segment Clock: two segments from a botched extraction, one from a triggered Complication Clause, one from a loose-lipped contact. At four of six, the GM has Wing security start showing up a beat early to nearby jobs — not the full response yet, but a clear signal.

## 6. Social Conflict

Social Conflict uses the same engine as any other roll: Attribute+Skill pool, opposed where there's a resisting party, resolved on the standard five outcome bands (Part II §2).

**Preparation pays off directly.** Before a social conflict, either side may spend a preparation action — an Investigation, Streetwise, or Corporate Knowledge roll made ahead of time. On a Success or better, that preparation grants one bonus die on the next roll in the related conflict, exactly like an E.G.O. die (adds to the pool), but drawn from outside the character — no Stress cost, nothing left to track once it's spent. If the preparation was built on a bluff instead of a true fact (a Deception roll rather than Investigation, Streetwise, or Corporate Knowledge), the bonus die still works, but risks a Complication if the bluff is ever tested in the same scene.

**Declare stakes first.** Before any social conflict roll: what does a full Success get, what does a Partial cost, what does Failure actually change. Say it out loud before the dice are rolled.

The engine covers four common flavors, distinguished by intent rather than mechanics:

- **Negotiation** — reshaping a deal, a contract, an agreement. Presence or Mind, paired with Persuasion.
- **Pressure** — compliance through fear, exposed information, or force of will. Presence, paired with Persuasion (or Empathy, used coldly).
- **Deception** — getting someone to believe something false. Presence, paired with Deception.
- **Commerce** — the exchange of money, goods, or services. Same engine as Negotiation, but the stakes are almost always price, not scope or safety.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A character tries to talk a Syndicate lieutenant down from a debt collection. She spent an earlier scene digging into his file — an Investigation roll, Success: he's skimming from his own boss. Stakes declared: full success buys a month's extension and the debt frozen; partial buys the extension but he wants something in return later; failure means he calls it in immediately. Presence 3 + Persuasion 2, five dice, plus the bonus die from her prep — six dice. She rolls a Partial: the extension, but he'll remember what she didn't say out loud — and so will the GM.

## 7. The Crew

The PCs usually work as a **crew**, attached to one Office (or, rarely, straight to an Association). A crew has its own sheet (Appendix I) for the things the group shares.

**The Office.** The crew's Office has a Function, a Grind and a Person Behind the Desk (Part VII §5). Its **Office Grade** is the best Grade among the crew's PCs. The Office Grade sets the highest Risk it is offered: **Grade 9 to 8, Risk 1 to 2; Grade 7 to 5, Risk 3; Grade 4 to 2, Risk 4; Grade 1, anything.**

**The Crew Bond.** The crew holds a Bond on itself: Trust or Obligation, Strength 1 to 3, starting at 2. Any member may spend a point to add a die to a roll that another member is Helping. It is restored by a Tend a Bond scene (Part VIII §10) with every member present. If the Crew Bond falls to 0 the crew is **Fractured**: no Help rolls between members until a scene with everyone present restores it to 1.

**The Fund.** A shared pool of Resources, 0 to 5. Members may put their own Resources into it in downtime, one for one. The crew spends it by agreement, mostly on Assets.

**Assets.** Bought from the Fund; each is a thing the crew owns together:

- **Safehouse** (Cost 3): a place to recover. Members recover up to 3 E.G.O. between Contracts instead of 2.
- **Getaway** (Cost 2): a vehicle or a route out. In a chase (Part VI §7), the pursued remove a segment when they win an Exchange by 1 or more, not 2.
- **Workshop contact** (Cost 2): repairs Spent gear for free, and takes 1 off the Cost of one piece of gear each downtime phase (minimum 1).
- **Informant** (Cost 2): a source. The preparation die (§6) is two dice instead of one.
- **Clinic** (Cost 2): treatment rolls (Part VI §2) are one Difficulty lower.
- **Cover** (Cost 2): a front business. Once per Contract, the first Heat segment you would fill on a faction's Clock is not filled.

With three or more Assets the Fund pays **1** each downtime phase to keep them up. If it can't, the newest Asset goes dormant until it can.

**Shared Heat and standing.** Heat Clocks belong to the crew: a botched job fills the crew's Clock with that faction, whoever rolled. The crew's standing with an institution is its **best member's** Standing (§2); one member may call on it for the crew once per Contract by spending the Bond underneath.

**The Ledger.** The crew keeps a record of each Contract: client, Risk, Payment, and whether the Report was honest or falsified. It is what the Association reads (Part VII §3), and the source for promotion (Part IX §1) and for the Heat on a falsified Report.

> **EXAMPLE**
> The crew (Wren, Dax, Lena, Tomas) is Grade 9 and offered Risk 1 or 2 jobs until Lena reaches Grade 7, when Risk 3 opens for the whole Office. Each puts one Resource in the Fund after the Market Street Shipment; after a second Contract they have 6, capped at 5, and buy a Safehouse (3). The Open War Clock with the Kurokumo Clan is the crew's: Dax's knife work and Tomas's talk both feed the same Clock.

Part VI covers what happens when Social Conflict isn't enough, and violence becomes the answer.
