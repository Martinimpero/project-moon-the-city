# PART VI — DANGER

Violence is rare in this game, and built to stay that way. When it happens, it should mean something — this Part covers Harm, Combat, and how both stay connected to everything else a character carries.

## 1. When Combat Starts

A scene becomes Combat only when a character takes an action meant to cause physical Harm, or credibly threatens to, and someone is both willing and able to resist by force. Everything short of that — a standoff, a threat, a chase without blows — stays in Social Conflict (Part V §6) or a single core roll (Part II §1).

**GM Advice.** Say it out loud when a scene crosses into Combat, the same way you declare stakes before any consequential roll: what's actually at risk, and what ends the fight.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A confrontation with a Syndicate enforcer in a Backstreets alley stays a Pressure roll right up until someone draws a blade — at which point the GM names it: "this is a fight now," and asks what each side is actually trying to accomplish.

## 2. Harm

Characters don't have Hit Points. Instead, physical injuries are recorded on a four-tier **Harm track**, filled in order as a character takes damage in a scene the GM has framed as genuinely dangerous.

1. **Hurt** — bruises, winding, a scare. Purely narrative; no mechanical penalty.
2. **Injured** — a real wound. −1 die to any roll the injury plausibly interferes with, until treated.
3. **Wounded** — serious harm. −2 die penalty, same condition. Requires real medical attention to stop worsening.
4. **Maimed / Dying** — the character is out of the scene. The GM and player negotiate the specific permanent consequence — a lost sense, a lasting scar, a function that doesn't come back — or, if the fiction supports it, character death.

There is no damage roll. A landed hit advances Harm one tier (§4). In a fight the GM framed as genuinely dangerous, an attack that wins by **+4 or more** advances it two. A Partial lands as a Complication and no Harm. Armor and cover work through the attack roll, not the Harm track (see §4).

**Healing.** Harm heals one tier at a time, from the top. **Hurt** clears at the end of the scene. **Injured** needs treatment (Mind or Resolve + Medicine, Difficulty 1, by anyone with a kit or a clinic), after which it heals one tier each downtime phase; untreated, it stays. **Wounded** gets worse if it isn't treated by the end of the next scene: Medicine, Difficulty 2, Help allowed. On a Success it is stabilized and then heals one tier per downtime phase; on a Failure the next hit puts the character at Maimed. **Maimed / Dying** is out of the scene. Someone must treat the character by the end of the next scene (Medicine, Difficulty 3) or they die. On a Success they survive at Wounded, plus a **lasting consequence** the player and GM name together (a lost sense, a limp, a hand that doesn't close right). Wing Support (Part I §3) heals one extra tier per downtime phase. A character who takes another hit while Maimed is dead.

> **EXAMPLE**
> Two Fixers are ambushed. One fails a Body+Athletics roll (Difficulty 2, one Success) trying to get clear of the first exchange. The ambusher's attack is a Critical Success, and the GM had framed this fight as dangerous going in, so Harm advances two tiers: from unhurt straight to Injured.

## 3. The Exchange

Combat resolves in **Exchanges**, not fixed turns. In each Exchange, every character involved takes one significant action, in an order the GM sets from the fiction — whoever's approach makes narrative sense goes first (an ambush, a faster draw, a desperate lunge); ties default to whichever player makes the best one-line case for acting first. There is no separate Initiative roll or stat. An Exchange ends when everyone involved has acted once; the GM narrates the beat as a whole before the next Exchange begins.

> **EXAMPLE**
> Three Fixers corner a lone Syndicate lookout who wasn't supposed to be there. The GM decides the startled lookout doesn't act first — the Exchange opens with a Fixer's action, then the lookout's desperate response, then the other two Fixers.

## 4. Attacks and Defense

An attack is an opposed roll: the attacker rolls Body (melee/thrown) or Mind (aimed ranged) + Combat; the defender rolls Body + Athletics to evade, or Body + Combat to actively counter or block — whichever fits how the player describes defending — or, if genuinely unable to respond, takes the result without rolling. The attacker needs more Successes than the defender to land cleanly; read the margin by Part II §2 (a tie or a one-Success deficit is a Partial: a Complication or a Hurt, not a clean hit). If the defender genuinely can't respond, there is no opposed roll: set a Difficulty as for any other attempt. An attack may be tagged with a Sin like any roll (Part X §3); the target's Alignment sets the matchup.

On a landed attack, the outcome band sets the effect:

- **Partial** — a Complication, often just closing distance or losing an advantage. No Harm, unless the fiction makes a wound unavoidable (then Hurt).
- **Success** — Harm advances one tier.
- **Critical Success** — Harm advances one tier, plus a serious extra effect: disarmed, pinned, exposed. In a fight framed as dangerous, a margin of +4 or more advances Harm two tiers instead.
- **Critical Failure** on the attacker's roll always means something goes wrong for them — never just nothing happening.

Armor and cover don't add a separate stat — they raise the bar to land a hit against whoever has them: the defender adds one die to their pool on an opposed roll, or the Difficulty rises by 1 if the defender can't respond.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A Fixer swings a length of pipe at an already-staggered lookout. Body 3 + Combat 2, five dice, against the lookout's Body 2 + Athletics 1, three dice. The Fixer rolls three Successes; the lookout rolls one. The attack lands as a Success: Harm advances from Hurt to Injured.

**GM Advice.** Not every scuffle needs the full opposed-roll treatment. Resolve a lopsided or low-stakes exchange with a single roll and a clear consequence instead.

## 5. Equipment

Weapons and armor sort into broad categories, not itemized stat blocks:

- **Improvised** — whatever's at hand. No bonus, but always available.
- **Light** — knives, batons, sidearms. Quick, concealable, no Harm bonus, rarely a Complication to carry.
- **Heavy** — rifles, blunt weapons, anything that draws attention. A bonus die on the attack roll, but a Complication risk if used somewhere it doesn't belong.
- **Abnormality-touched** — objects or weapons connected to something not fully human. Treated case by case, almost always carrying its own Trauma or Stress risk to use rather than a clean mechanical bonus.

Armor doesn't add a Harm buffer directly — it raises the bar to land a hit against its wearer, per §4.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A character with an Abnormality-touched blade uses it as a Heavy weapon with an extra bonus die — but every use is a Resolve roll to avoid raising Stress, and a second consecutive use in the same scene risks feeding directly into a Hail Mary.

### Resources and Cost

Money is a single rating, **Resources**, from 0 to 5. A character starts with **2**. When a Contract pays out, every PC earns **Resources equal to its Risk** (minus 1 if the Contract came through an Office and was Risk 3 or 4: the Office's cut). Each downtime phase costs **1** (rent, taxes, upkeep: Part VIII §10). Resources never go above 5.

Anything you buy has a **Cost**, paid from Resources: **1** for armor, a Light weapon, or a medical kit; **2** for a Heavy weapon, a piece of attuned gear, or a Backstreets augmentation; **3** for Fine attuned gear or a Nest augmentation; **4** for a Workshop to extract E.G.O. gear (Compendium). If you can't pay, you may **borrow the shortfall** as a Debt Bond to whoever sells it (Strength equal to the shortfall, up to 3). Small purchases happen in the fiction; anything of Cost 2 or more is a downtime action.

### Attuned Gear

Some gear is made from what a Sin leaves behind, and it works best in the hands of someone who already carries that Sin. A piece of **attuned gear** has one **Sin** and a **Boon**. There are four kinds:

- **Weapon:** +1 die on attacks tagged with its Sin.
- **Suit** (a coat, a jacket, plates): +1 die on defense rolls tagged with its Sin.
- **Tool:** +1 die on a roll tagged with its Sin that uses one of that Sin's Favored Skills (Part X §4).
- **Charm:** once per scene, ignore the Sin's Complication from an E.G.O. die.

A piece costs **2**. **Fine** gear costs **3** and its Boon is +2 if you are attuned at rating 3 or more.

> **EXAMPLE**
> The *Ember Knife* (Wrath, Weapon) adds a die to Wrath-tagged attacks. The *Dustcoat* (Sloth, Suit) adds a die to a defense roll tagged Sloth: *I don't move; let it land on the coat.*

**Attuned or borrowed.** You are **attuned** to a piece if your Resonance in its Sin is 2 or more: you tag it as normal. If your rating is 0 or 1 you can still **borrow** it. With the gear in hand you may tag its Sin on rolls that use the gear *as if your rating were 2* (the matchup swing is ±1 and the Boon applies). Borrowing gives no Fit, no Riding and no Signature Technique. It is how someone with no Wrath hits like Wrath on the one night they need to. It is also how a Sin gets its hooks in.

**Limits.** Only one Boon applies to a roll (the best, if attuned gear and E.G.O. gear both apply). All Sin-derived dice on a roll together (matchup, Fit, Boon) never exceed **+4**. You can use **two** pieces of attuned gear at once; with three or more, they all Pull on every 1.

**The price: Pull, Tally and Wear.**

- **Pull.** Attuned gear provokes its Sin. The first time each scene a roll using the gear shows a 1, the Sin's Complication (Part X §7) lands on that roll as if an E.G.O. die had shown it. If you are borrowing, or Under that Sin, or Overtaken in it, *every* 1 Pulls.
- **Tally.** Every roll you tag through the gear puts a mark on that Sin in your Sin Tally (two marks if you are borrowing). Carry Wrath gear long enough and you drift toward Wrath (Part X §6).
- **Wear.** Each Pull puts one **Wear** on the gear. At 3 Wear it is **Spent**: no Boon until you pay 1 Resources and a downtime action to repair it.

### Augmentation

An augmentation adds **+1 die** to rolls of one Skill. A **Nest** augmentation costs 3 and is clean. A **Backstreets** augmentation costs 2, but the first 1 shown on a roll that uses it each scene brings a Complication (a malfunction), and it comes with a Debt Bond of Strength 1 to whoever fitted it. Only the brain stays organic.

## 6. Group and Environmental Combat

A group of minor opponents — guards, mooks, a Syndicate crew — is run as a single **Threat** with its own small track (Holding / Breaking / Routed), rather than individual characters with separate Harm tracks. Actions against the Threat chip away at that track collectively, and the Threat acts as one unit each Exchange. A single named, dangerous opponent still uses the normal Harm track.

A Success against the Threat moves it down one stage (Holding→Breaking, or Breaking→Routed); a Critical Success moves it down two stages at once.

**Solo named opponents facing a full party.** A sufficiently dangerous named opponent (Threat Grade 4 or lower) gets one additional defensive response each Exchange — a second Defense roll against a different attacker, or a single counter-Harm reply against whoever just landed a hit. This is not a second action or an extra turn, and it doesn't stack with itself; it exists so one dangerous opponent isn't reduced to a single Exchange purely by being outnumbered.

Environmental danger — fire, structural collapse, a crowd turning hostile — is run as a Clock (Part II §4) that fills from Complications and bad rolls during the fight, and which the GM narrates concretely when it fills: the floor gives way, the crowd surges.

> **EXAMPLE**
> Three Syndicate enforcers converge on a Fixer crew. The GM runs them as one Threat at Holding (Grade 8 muscle, so the group rolls 5 dice: Part IX §4). A Critical Success against them pushes the Threat straight to Breaking; at Routed, the fight is over — the enforcers scatter or drop, whichever the fiction supports.

## 7. Retreat and Concession

Any character may **concede** at any point before an Exchange resolves. The fight ends immediately for them, on terms the GM sets — worse than winning, but survivable: captured, disarmed, forced to give something up. No further Harm is taken.

A character may instead try to **retreat**: an opposed roll (Body+Athletics or similar, against whatever's trying to stop them from leaving), not an automatic exit. Failing to retreat costs a further Exchange still inside the fight.

**Chases.** A chase that matters is a Clock of 4 to 6 segments, by distance. Each Exchange the pursuer and the pursued roll an opposed pair (Body+Athletics, or whatever fits the route). If the pursuer wins, fill a segment; if the pursued win by 2 or more, remove one. When it fills, the pursued are caught and the fight (or a concession) resumes. They escape if they win three Exchanges in a row, or break the pursuit through the fiction (a locked door, a crowd, the third wave of the Night). An Abnormality in pursuit rolls a pool by Class: ZAYIN or TETH 3, HE 4, WAW 6, ALEPH 8.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A Fixer whose Harm has reached Wounded, facing a hit that would push to Maimed, concedes instead. The scene ends with her disarmed and pinned — humiliated, but alive.

**GM Advice.** Make concession terms interesting and survivable, not humiliating for its own sake, or players will avoid the option entirely. A losing position should never have to end in death by default.

## 8. Combat and the Self

Combat is never mechanically separate from Part IV.

Landing a serious blow, or causing real Harm to another person, raises Stress by 1 for the character who did it, at most once per scene — unless that specific act is already inside their Vice or otherwise fictionally "clean" for them (a trained soldier doesn't flinch the way a Wing researcher does). Taking Harm that reaches Wounded or worse is itself a Trauma-check moment: if it hits a character's Boundary, it fires exactly like any other Trauma trigger. E.G.O. dice can be spent on Combat rolls exactly as anywhere else, and if a character's E.G.O. would hit 0 at a moment that hits their Boundary mid-fight, the Hail Mary triggers in the middle of Combat, resolved before the Exchange continues.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A character with a Boundary against killing is forced into a fight where the only way to stop an opponent is lethal force. Her E.G.O. is already at 1 — spending her last die here would hit 0 at exactly the moment that violates her Boundary. The Hail Mary triggers mid-Exchange. She rolls a Partial: for one beat, something colder and faster than her finishes the fight in her place, and she claws back control only once it's already over.

## 9. Worked Example

A Fixer crew is caught mid-extraction by a Syndicate patrol — three enforcers plus their lieutenant. The GM calls it: "this is a fight now," and asks what each side wants. The Fixers want out with the package; the lieutenant wants them held until backup arrives.

The three enforcers run as a single Threat (Holding); the lieutenant runs on his own Harm track. Exchange one: a Fixer opens with a Body+Combat roll against an enforcer's defense, landing a Success — the Threat drops to Breaking. The lieutenant grabs the crew's youngest member, threatening her — no roll yet, just fiction raising the stakes. Exchange two: her player chooses to concede on her behalf rather than escalate, buying the crew a clean exit at the cost of the package being confiscated.

The fight ends in two Exchanges. Nobody reaches Wounded. The crew leaves with a new Debt owed to whoever they call to get the package back.
