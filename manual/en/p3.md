# PART III — CHARACTERS

## 1. Attributes

Every character has four Attributes, each rated 1 (poor) to 5 (exceptional). A rating of 5 is never available at character creation — it's earned in play (Part IX).

- **Body** — physical capacity: strength, endurance, reflexes, pain tolerance.
- **Mind** — reasoning, perception, technical and analytical thought.
- **Presence** — outward social force: persuasion, projection, threat, how a character lands on other people.
- **Resolve** — inward steadiness: what holds a character together under pressure, and what they draw on to resist Stress.

Skills aren't locked to a single Attribute. A Skill pairs with whichever Attribute fits what the character is actually doing — Empathy+Mind to clinically read someone's state, Empathy+Resolve to sit with someone in their grief without flinching. The GM sets the pairing out loud, per roll, based on how the player describes the action.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A player wants to talk their way past a Wing receptionist who's been told to stonewall everyone. If the player plays it straight — making a genuine case, projecting confidence — that's Persuasion+Presence. If they lie about who they are, that's Deception+Presence. The GM picks the pairing from what the player actually said, not from what's optimal on the sheet.

## 2. Skills

Twelve Skills, each rated 0 (untrained) to 5 (expert). Untrained doesn't mean a character can't attempt something — it means the roll uses the Attribute alone.

**Combat, Athletics, Stealth, Investigation, Technology, Medicine, Persuasion, Deception, Empathy, Streetwise, Corporate Knowledge, Fixer Knowledge.**

- *Combat* covers both melee and ranged violence — the weapon in the character's hand changes the framing and Attribute pairing (Body+Combat for a knife, Mind+Combat for a called shot at range), not the Skill.
- *Technology* covers both electronics/hacking and mechanical/structural work.
- *Investigation* covers both digging for a clue over time and simply noticing one in the moment.
- *Persuasion* covers both making a case and applying pressure — what changes is the fictional framing, not the Skill.
- *Corporate Knowledge* and *Fixer Knowledge* are kept deliberately separate: they mark which of the City's two dominant worlds — Wing corporate life, or the Fixer/Association underworld — a character actually comes from.

## 3. Character Creation

New to the City? Read **Before You Build** first: it names some places to be from. Then build a character in this order. Later steps build on earlier ones, so don't skip ahead.

1. **Concept.** One line combining Identity and Occupation. *"A Backstreets medic who used to work Wing security."*
2. **Identity.** A short self-concept phrase, not a class. *"The one who fixes people, not machines."*
3. **Background.** Where the character is from. May grant one free Skill point tied to that upbringing, at the GM's discretion.
4. **Occupation.** Pick or build a Skill package, using the point-buy and package list below.
5. **Affiliation.** An Office (where most Fixers work), a Wing, a Syndicate, an Association, a mutual-aid network, or none. Grants a starting Bond.
6. **Relationship.** Name one person who matters to this character. Grants a Bond.
7. **Principle.** One belief, stated as something that can bend under real pressure. Purely fictional — it has no dedicated tracked stat, but the GM can bring it up under pressure exactly as they would a Vice, and it should visibly inform how the character acts.
8. **Your Wound.** Answer three questions together, as one thing, not three: *What happened to you? What are you afraid of because of it? What line will you never cross again because of it?* The first answer is the **Burden** — a concrete past event, written so it can trigger again in the fiction. The second is the **Fear**, specific enough to name — not "dying," but "dying without anyone knowing what happened to me." The third is the **Boundary** — personal, not a setting-wide taboo, not "I won't hurt kids" but "I won't let someone die on my table because I hesitated." This is the fault line the Hail Mary (Part IV) keys off. Write all three from the same wound in one sitting; if any of them feels like it belongs to a different character, revise until it doesn't. The event itself becomes the character's starting Trauma automatically — there's no separate step for it. Then ask **what that Wound feels like** and spend 6 points of **Resonance** in the Sins (Part X §2); your highest Sin is your Alignment. Write the Burden at the level of detail you want to put on the table; the table needs a trigger, not the worst version (Part VIII §11).
9. **Desire.** A concrete, pursuable want the GM can put pressure on.
10. **Vice.** A recurring, specific weakness, detailed below.
11. **Ambition.** The question this character's whole story should eventually answer.
12. **Attributes.** Spend the point-buy below.
13. **Skills.** Spend the point-buy below.
14. **Derive.** Resonance (6 points across the seven Sins; the highest is your Alignment), E.G.O. maximum, starting Stress, the Self track, starting Bonds, and one piece of gear — see below.

## 4. Attributes & Skills Point-Buy

**Attributes.** All four start at 1. Distribute 6 additional points among them, maximum 4 per Attribute at creation — 10 dots total. (5 is never available at creation.)

**Skills.** All twelve start at 0. Occupation grants a package: pick 3 Skills relevant to the Occupation and set each to 2. Then distribute 7 more points freely, maximum 3 per Skill at creation — 13 dots total between the package and the free points.

> **EXAMPLE**
> Body 2 / Mind 2 / Presence 2 / Resolve 4 is a valid Attribute spread. A medic's Occupation package sets Medicine 2, Streetwise 2, Empathy 2; the remaining 7 points push Medicine to 3 and add six other Skills at 1 each.

## 5. Occupation Packages

Not exhaustive — a template for building more. Each grants 3 Skills at 2.

| Occupation | Skill package |
|---|---|
| Fixer (any grade) | Streetwise, Fixer Knowledge, Combat or Stealth |
| Wing Researcher | Corporate Knowledge, Technology, Medicine or Investigation |
| Office Employee | Corporate Knowledge, Persuasion, Investigation |
| Backstreets Survivor | Streetwise, Athletics, one specialty (Stealth, Medicine, Technology) |
| Syndicate Member | Streetwise, Persuasion, Combat |
| Backstreets Medic (unlicensed) | Medicine, Streetwise, Empathy |
| Wing Security | Combat, Investigation, Persuasion |
| Independent Investigator | Investigation, Empathy, Deception or Persuasion |

## 6. Vice

A Vice is a specific, recurring weakness — "can't say no to a patient, even when it's obviously a trap," not "greedy." At any point the fiction makes it plausible, the GM may invoke a character's Vice to introduce a complication into the scene — the Vice creates the problem, not a die roll. In exchange, the player immediately regains 1 E.G.O., up to maximum. A player may also invoke their own Vice unprompted, for the same payout, with GM approval, as long as it creates a real complication. Whoever invokes it, a Vice pays out E.G.O. at most once per scene.

A Vice is also where a character's worst instinct lives once the pressure finally gets to them — see Flashpoint.

> **EXAMPLE**
> Mira's Vice is "can't say no to a patient." The GM has an obviously-compromised Fixer show up wounded, needing help right now, no questions asked. Mira's player says yes — that's who she is. The GM narrates the complication (word gets around that she treats people who shouldn't be treated), and Mira's player regains 1 E.G.O.

## 7. Identity Components

Every component of a character does real work at the table, whether or not it has a number attached to it — none of it is decoration. Fear, Boundary, and Burden are written together, as one wound, in step 8 above; they're listed separately here because each does distinct work afterward.

| Component | Question | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Who are you? | The seed for E.G.O. Manifestation and Distortion — both should visibly relate back to this phrase. |
| Background | Where did you come from? | Colors starting Bonds and which Occupations make sense; may grant one free Skill point. |
| Occupation | How do you survive? | Grants a Skill package. Not a power tier — see Part IX for how this Occupation's institution tracks advancement. |
| Affiliation | Who do you work for? | Grants a starting Bond and sets initial standing with that institution. |
| Desire | What do you want? | The thing external pressure is built to threaten or dangle. |
| Fear | What are you afraid of? | The specific, nameable thing that justifies raising Stress — not "something scary happened." |
| Relationship | Who matters to you? | Grants a starting Bond. |
| Principle | What do you believe? | Purely fictional — no dedicated track, but the GM can invoke it under pressure the way a Vice gets invoked. |
| Vice | What weakness causes problems? | A GM-invokable complication with a mechanical payout, detailed above. |
| Boundary | What will you never do? | The character's fault line — sets the trigger condition for the Hail Mary. |
| Resonance | What does the Wound feel like? | Ratings in the seven Sins; the highest is your Alignment. Drives matchups, Fit, and the shape of your E.G.O. and Distortion (Part X). |
| Burden | What do you carry? | Seeds one starting Trauma, written as a concrete trigger condition. |
| Ambition | What would make life worth living? | The campaign-long throughline. Institutional advancement (Part IX) is built to pay it off directly. |

## 8. Seeding E.G.O., Stress, and Bonds

- **Self track:** exactly 1 Boundary at creation (Part IV §3 covers how a second one can arrive).
- **Resonance:** 6 points across the seven Sins, maximum 3 in any one (Part X §2).
- **E.G.O. maximum** equals the character's Resolve score. Starting E.G.O. is full.
- **Starting Stress:** 1, because the Burden-derived Trauma already touches the same fault line as the Boundary, per the Wound in §3 step 8.
- **Starting Bonds:** 2 — one from Relationship (Type chosen by the player, Strength 2), one from Affiliation (usually Obligation or Debt, Strength 1). A Background may grant a third, minor Bond (Strength 1) at the GM's discretion.
- **Resources and gear:** Resources 2. One piece of gear of Cost 2 or less, which may be a piece of attuned gear (Part VI §5), so a character can start with a Sin already in hand.

## 9. Character Sheet Fields

Identity · Background · Occupation · Affiliation · Desire · Fear · Relationship · Principle · Vice · Boundary · Burden · Ambition — **Attributes** (Body, Mind, Presence, Resolve) — **Skills** (12, 0–5) — **E.G.O.** (current/max) — **Stress** (0–5) — **Self track**: 1 Boundary — **Harm** (4 tiers) — **Bonds** (Type + Strength, at least 2) — **Trauma** (starts with 1, from Burden) — **Scars** (from Distortion, if any) — **Institutional Grade** (starts at 9 — Fixer Grade, Wing Tier, Syndicate Rank, Civil Grade, or Renown, depending on Occupation; see Part IX). Also **Resonance** (the seven Sins, 0–4), **Alignment**, and a **Sin Tally** (Part X §6). Also **Resources** (0–5) and **Gear** (with its Sin and Wear).

A blank character sheet is in the Appendix.
