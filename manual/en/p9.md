# PART IX — ADVANCEMENT

Every character in the City is ranked by whichever institution they actually answer to — the same 9-to-1 scale, the same four reward breakpoints, under a different name depending on where the character stands. Whatever it's called at your table, it works exactly as described below; only the fiction of who assigns it changes.

| Occupation | Track name | Assigned by |
|---|---|---|
| Fixer | Grade | Their Association |
| Wing employee | Tier | Department hierarchy |
| Syndicate member | Rank | Crew leadership |
| Office employee | Civil Grade | Seniority review |
| Independent | Renown | No formal body — reputation alone |

The rest of this Part uses "Fixer Grade" as the worked example throughout, since it's the most common case — read "Grade" as shorthand for whichever track above actually applies to a given character.

## 1. Fixer Grade

Fixers and Offices in the City are ranked by their Associations on a numerical scale from **Grade 9** (lowest — barely more capable than an untrained civilian) to **Grade 1** (highest — trusted with jobs that verge on taking down a Star of the City).

Characters normally begin at Grade 9, unless the table agrees a Background specifically justifies starting at Grade 8.

A Grade rises through the fiction: completing Contracts at a given Risk rating, tending Standing (Part V §2) with an Association, and a proving moment the GM and table agree actually crossed a threshold — cross-referenced against the Association's own in-fiction judgment.

**The Report.** What an Association reads is the Report that ends each Contract (Part VII §3). An honest Report on a Risk 2 or higher Contract counts toward promotion as described below. A falsified one counts as two Contracts, but opens a Heat Clock (4) on the Association.

**GM Advice.** As a guideline: three Risk-2-or-higher Contracts, or one clean Risk-3, is usually enough for an Association to consider a Grade 9 for promotion to Grade 8. Each subsequent Grade generally asks for higher-Risk work, deeper Standing, or a genuinely defining moment.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A Fixer crew has spent six sessions running Risk 2 jobs for the Hana Association and cultivating Standing there. After a Risk 3 job that ends with the Kurokumo Clan's lieutenant personally vouching for one of them — an enemy's respect, not a friend's — the GM rules that's the proving moment. The Association formally reviews her file and bumps her from Grade 9 to Grade 8. Nothing on her character sheet changes except a word — but every NPC who reads her file now treats her differently.

## 2. Grade Rewards

Mechanical unlocks land at four specific breakpoints, not every Grade. Each unlocks exactly one dot in one place the player chooses.

- **Grade 7** — unlock Skill 4 in one Skill already at 3.
- **Grade 5** — unlock Attribute 5 in one Attribute already at 4.
- **Grade 3** — unlock Skill 5 (mastery) in one Skill already at 4.
- **Grade 1** — no further dots. Risk 4 Contracts become standard work rather than exceptional jobs, and Standing with every major institution starts at Known by default, City-wide.

Between breakpoints, Grade still matters — better Contracts, real institutional deference, doors that open — it just doesn't add a dot to the sheet every time it rises. These breakpoints apply on any institutional track from the table above — a Wing Researcher at Tier 5 unlocks Attribute 5 exactly as a Fixer at Grade 5 does.

> **EXAMPLE**
> A character reaches Grade 5 after a campaign-defining Risk 3 job. The player chooses to unlock Resolve 5 — the character's whole arc has been about holding together under pressure, and the sheet should say so.

## 3. Color

Rarely, a Grade 1 Fixer who has gone well beyond ordinary excellence is given a **Color** — a personal title assigned by their Association, marking them as one of the very few Fixers trusted and capable enough to move independently, outside Office assignment, and to be sent after Stars of the City.

Reaching Color requires all three, together:

- **Grade 1.**
- An existing **Permanent E.G.O.** — earned either through a second Manifestation on the same fault line, or through Integrating a Healed Scar.
- A **defining feat** the table and GM agree on — almost always surviving or resolving a Star of the City–tier confrontation, below.

Canon confirms Color Fixers exist and are named by the Hana Association for renown beyond Grade 1 — the exact criteria above are this game's own mechanical answer to that renown, not a claim about how the Association actually decides.

When a character reaches Color, the table names them together — a title tied to their manifestation and their Identity phrase. Their Permanent E.G.O. no longer costs 1 E.G.O. to activate — it's simply who they are now — and their Standing is effectively Made everywhere in the City without needing to be earned district by district.

> **EXAMPLE**
> Mira Sato, whose Permanent E.G.O. *Steady Hands* became fixed after a second Manifestation, spends a full campaign arc climbing to Grade 1 through Wing-adjacent medical Contracts. The arc closes when she single-handedly stabilizes a mass-casualty Abnormality breach that a Star of the City caused in passing — not defeating the Star, just refusing to let it cost more lives than it already had. The table names her *The Steady Hand* on the spot. *Steady Hands* now activates for free.

## 4. Threat Grade

Any named opposition can be given a **Threat Grade** on the same 9–1 scale, mapped directly onto Difficulty:

| Threat Grade | Difficulty | Dice it rolls | Typical treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9–8 | 1 | 3 | A single mook, or folded into a Threat track (Part VI §6). |
| 7–5 | 2 | 4 | A capable individual, or a Threat-tracked group. |
| 4–2 | 3 | 6 | A named opponent with a full Harm track (Part VI §2). |
| 1 | 4 | 8 | A named opponent expected to require a full crew, not a solo PC. |

**Dice it rolls.** When a Threat can respond, it rolls this pool in the opposed roll instead of the Difficulty applying; a Threat track (a group) rolls the pool of its Grade +2. A hit from a Threat advances Harm one tier. Name an NPC's pool once and keep it. Its Sin rating comes from Grade too (Part X §3).

A Threat Grade is a starting point, not a hard rule — frame a Grade 3 threat as more dangerous than its number suggests if the fiction earns it: an ambush, terrain, numbers.

> **EXAMPLE**
> The Kurokumo Clan's lieutenant is rated Threat Grade 4 — a genuine named opponent, Difficulty 3, expected to threaten a lone PC seriously but not overwhelm a coordinated crew.

## 5. Star of the City

Star of the City is the Head's own classification for the most dangerous threats in the City — and, just as often, the most dangerous people and Syndicates, since the Five Fingers carry the label too. What actually defines the rank isn't raw power: it's that the Head has looked at the threat and chosen not to act. Below, "Star of the City" is used for the case that matters most at the table — a single overwhelming entity — and it sits above the Grade 1–9 scale entirely. Felling one is what Color Fixers exist for. It is never resolved through a single roll or a single Exchange sequence.

Instead, build a **Confrontation** out of multiple Clocks: typically one representing the Star's active threat to the City directly, and two or three representing what the crew must do first to even have a chance against it — expose a weakness, cut off a resource, evacuate what it would otherwise destroy. Only once the preparatory Clocks are filled does a final Exchange sequence against the Star itself become winnable at all; attempting it early is legal, but should read in the fiction as close to suicidal. A Star of the City confrontation is built to need a full, coordinated crew — solo PCs, even Grade 1 ones, are not expected to face one alone.

> **EXAMPLE**
> *The Choir That Wasn't*, a Star of the City active in the Backstreets. The Confrontation: a "City Awareness" Clock (8 segments) fills as it grows bolder regardless of PC action; a "Weakness Exposed" Clock (6 segments) fills through Investigation and Insight-Approach Abnormality work; an "Evacuation" Clock (4 segments) fills through Social Conflict and Contract work protecting Backstreets residents. Only once Weakness Exposed and Evacuation are both full does the GM allow a final Exchange sequence against the Star itself to begin.

## 6. Worked Example

A campaign's arc, compressed: a Fixer crew starts at Grade 9, running Risk 1–2 Contracts through the Hana Association. By session eight, steady work and a proving moment with the Kurokumo Clan earns two of them Grade 8, then Grade 7 — one unlocks Streetwise 4, the other Combat 4. A mid-campaign arc built around a Wing's internal Secret pushes the medic of the group to Grade 5; she unlocks Resolve 5, the same Attribute already carrying her Hail Mary odds.

The campaign's climax is a Star of the City Confrontation threatening the district they've spent the whole campaign inside. Filling the preparatory Clocks draws on nearly everything in this book — Investigation and Insight-Approach work against the Star's nature, Social Conflict and Contracts to evacuate residents, and a final Exchange sequence that costs the crew real Harm but not their lives. The medic, already Grade 1 with a Permanent E.G.O. from an earlier Hail Mary arc, is the one who stabilizes the worst of the evacuation casualties in the confrontation's final beat. The table names her Color on the spot: *The Steady Hand*.

## 7. Growth Through Play

Grade is what the City thinks of you; **Marks** are how a character actually gets better. At the end of each Contract, every player earns **1 Mark**, plus **1** more if the Contract was Risk 3 or 4, plus **1** more (once per session) for a played Reckoning or a scene that grew a Bond.

Spend Marks in downtime, in a scene that shows the training or the practice:

- **Raise a Skill by 1:** the cost is the new rating (0 to 1 costs 1; 2 to 3 costs 3). Cap 3, until the Grade rewards in §2 raise it.
- **Raise an Attribute by 1:** the cost is three times the new rating (1 to 2 costs 6). Cap 4, until Grade 5.

Raising Resolve raises the E.G.O. maximum by 1. Expect about two Marks a session: a Skill point every session or two early on, an Attribute point every four to six.
